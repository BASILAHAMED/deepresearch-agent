# Sodium-Ion Battery Technology for Electric Vehicles in India: Current State

## 1. Executive Summary

Sodium-ion (Na-ion) technology for Indian EVs is at an early, pre-commercial stage. The most concrete activity is Reliance's acquisition of the UK firm Faradion, but the sources do not confirm any Indian sodium-ion EV deployment or verified cell production. Because this research relied mainly on search-result snippets and headlines, with a source quality score of 42%, the findings are preliminary and the overall confidence is **Low**.

## 2. Key Findings

### 2.1 Corporate Activity and Ownership

- **Reliance's acquisition of Faradion.** Reliance New Energy Solar signed a definitive agreement to acquire 100% of Faradion, described as a leading global sodium-ion battery developer [29]. A secondary source reports a price of about $135 million [30], but this figure needs confirmation from a primary filing or announcement.
- **Faradion's India plans.** A trade report says Faradion was looking to set up a battery manufacturing plant in India, focused on sodium-ion cells [4]. This describes intent. The sources do not confirm that a plant was built or is operating.
- **Reliance's EV battery plant.** Reliance has been linked to a battery gigafactory expected to begin operations in the second half of 2026 [25][26]. It also secured 10 GWh of cell production capacity under a national incentive scheme [21]. Its record is mixed: it reportedly sought an extension for its EV battery plant under the PLI scheme [27][28], and one headline says Reliance paused its lithium-ion push amid a China technology hurdle [23]. It is not clear whether the gigafactory plans involve sodium-ion chemistry.
- **Domestic start-ups.** IndiEnergy describes itself as "India's first sodium-ion battery company" and says it converts agricultural waste into sodium-ion battery materials [5]. This is company self-description. The sources contain no independent verification of its technology, scale, or customers.
- **Global context.** CATL reportedly invested close to 10 billion yuan in sodium-ion R&D by the end of 2025 [1]. Headlines also indicate CATL plans commercial-scale sodium-ion EV battery deployment in 2026 [14] and a 40 GWh sodium-ion push [1]. Whether Indian OEMs can access this supply is not addressed.

### 2.2 Market Projections

- One market analysis projects sodium-ion capacity rising from about 70 GWh in 2025 to about 400 GWh by 2030, while describing the technology as in an early commercial stage with limited large-scale deployment compared with lithium-ion [6].
- A separate IESA projection puts India's total EV battery demand at about 200 GWh by 2032 [16]. This covers all EV battery chemistries, not sodium-ion alone, so it provides context rather than a direct sodium-ion forecast.

### 2.3 Technology Advantages (Claimed)

- Sources describe sodium-ion as cheaper than lithium-ion, by 15–20% in one blog [3], and as using abundant sodium resources [3]. A 2019 Bridge India report argued that sodium-ion would be well suited to India's FAME II goals [8].
- The International Energy Agency (IEA) is cited as saying sodium-ion is moving toward commercial success, though lithium-ion, particularly LFP, still holds advantages [7].
- A Reddit thread claims a sodium-ion battery charges to 80% in six minutes [9]. This claim is unverified and should be treated with caution.

## 3. Evidence Analysis

**Where sources agree:**
- Sodium-ion is at an early commercial stage. The market analysis [6] and IEA-based commentary [7] both indicate that lithium-ion, especially LFP, remains dominant.
- Sodium-ion has an ideal policy and strategic fit in India given its resource profile and interest in domestic manufacturing [3][8][12].

**Where sources conflict or are unclear:**
- **Reliance timelines.** Sources give conflicting signals. Operations are expected in H2 2026 [25][26], but the company has sought an extension [27][28], was reportedly fined for missing a deadline (per the Business Standard URL in [28]), and reportedly paused its lithium-ion push [23]. These cannot all be reconciled from the available material.
- **Cost claims.** The 15–20% cost advantage [3] comes from a general-purpose blog with no methodology. It is not tied to Indian cell costs, import duties, or material prices.
- **Market size.** The 70→400 GWh sodium-ion projection [6] and the 200 GWh total EV demand figure [16] are not directly comparable, and neither is independently verified.
- **Indian EV sales.** A LinkedIn post cites 2 million EVs sold in FY2024–25 and a record of 2.45 million units [2]. The post does not clearly separate periods or vehicle categories, so these figures should not be used without checking official sources.

**Source quality:** Most sources are news headlines, search-result titles, marketing pages, blogs, social media posts, or market-research teasers. Several entries have no retrievable content beyond their title (e.g., [10], [11], [12], [13], [16], [22]). Only the snippets were available for review, so conclusions rest on limited text.

## 5. Gaps & Limitations

The coverage gaps identified during research are significant:

1. **No confirmed Indian sodium-ion EV deployments.** The sources do not show which OEMs (Tata, Mahindra, Ola, Maruti, or others) have launched or piloted sodium-ion vehicles.
2. **Unverified production status.** It is unknown whether any Indian facility is producing sodium-ion cells, or at what capacity. The Reliance–Faradion production status is not confirmed.
3. **Policy coverage not established.** The sources do not show whether PLI-ACC, FAME/PM E-DRIVE, or BIS/AIS standards explicitly cover sodium-ion or provide sodium-specific incentives or test protocols. The DST MoU [12] is mentioned only by title.
4. **No INR/kWh cost comparison.** No sources provide localized cost data, including import duty, hard-carbon anode, or cathode costs.
5. **No Indian-climate performance data.** Information on cold/heat performance, cycle life, and Wh/kg for two-wheelers, three-wheelers, or cars is absent.
6. **Limited academic and R&D detail.** No sources cover IISc, IITs, DRDO, or CSIR work on sodium-ion, and IndiEnergy's claims are unverified marketing material.
7. **Supply-chain analysis missing.** Sources do not address sodium availability in India, hard-carbon sourcing, or dependence on Chinese supply chains (CATL, HiNa).
8. **No recent verified news on CATL's EV rollout.** Headlines suggest 2026 deployment plans, but the sources do not confirm rollout status or Indian OEM access.
