# Sodium-Ion Battery Technology for Electric Vehicles in India: Current State

## 1. Executive Summary

Sodium-ion (Na-ion) batteries are a credible but still early alternative to lithium-ion for EVs, with the strongest case in cost, raw-material abundance, and cold-temperature performance, and a clear trade-off in energy density [1][7][10][12]. Indian-specific evidence is thin: the sources identify research activity, policy interest, and a hard-carbon-based supply chain opportunity [5][15][17][26][30], but they do not establish Indian manufacturing capacity, deployments, pricing, or regulation. The overall state of the technology is therefore reasonably characterized, while the current status of Indian commercialization remains largely unverified.

## 2. Key Findings

### 2.1 Technology Maturity and Materials

- **Hard carbon dominates the anode pathway.** Multiple sources identify hard carbon as the common anode material [1][17][24], with biomass-derived variants receiving significant attention for cost and sustainability [18][19]. One India-focused review describes hard carbon as the best candidate for the active anode [17].
- **Supply chain readiness is debated.** One source describes a well-established supply chain for sodium-ion battery materials [1], while another frames the question as one of supply-chain sustainability and mass-production readiness [16]. The evidence suggests materials are available but that scaling and sustainability are not yet solved.
- **Raw-material social and sourcing risks are emerging concerns.** A screening study notes that most raw materials for Na-ion batteries have low domestic production in the EU, and that biomass or pitch-based precursors may reduce dependence [25]. This is directly relevant to India, which faces similar import-dependence questions, though the source does not address India specifically.

### 2.2 Performance Trade-offs

- **Energy density is the main disadvantage.** Several sources converge on lower energy density compared with lithium-ion, including LFP [7][10][23]. Comparative work uses volumetric energy density and cost as core metrics [13].
- **Cold-temperature performance is a relative strength.** A study of Na-ion and Li-ion under dynamic EV driving cycles reports advantages for sodium-ion in cold weather, with operation reported down to about -20°C [6]. Low-temperature reviews identify both the challenges and strategies for improving performance [8][21], with one noting relevance to short-range EVs and grid storage [21].
- **Thermal and cycling stability.** Sodium-ion and LFP are described as having superior thermal and cycling stability relative to higher-energy chemistries, at the cost of lower energy density [10]. A thermal behavior review notes that safety is a major challenge for Li-ion and that sodium-ion must still be brought to a comparable level of maturity [2].

### 2.3 Commercialization

- **China is the most advanced industrial base in the sources.** A review of industrialization in China covers downstream sodium-ion supply chains and identifies current gaps [20].
- **Western commercialization precedent exists.** Faradion's commercialization journey is documented as a reference case for high-energy-density Na-ion cells [3].
- **India has policy-level commitments.** A DST announcement of an MoU is titled as aimed at helping commercialization of sodium-ion technology [26], and an Indian S&T portal lists sodium-ion and lithium-sulfur batteries with a status marked "Commercialised" [29]. However, the snippet of [29] is truncated and does not indicate what is commercialized, by whom, or at what scale.

### 2.4 India-Specific Research and Policy

- **Bibliometric evidence of Indian research activity.** A bibliometric study of India's role in sodium-ion technology highlights research on hard carbon and biomass-derived carbons, with early breakthroughs in cathode and anode materials [5]. Its citation count is low, so it should be treated as recent rather than established.
- **Policy-focused literature.** A 2026 article on policy-driven strategies for Na-ion adoption in India discusses advances in Prussian blue analogue cathodes and hard carbon, and frames the case against LIB risks such as raw-material cost and supply-chain exposure [15].
- **Supply chain framing.** An IISD report on localizing EV battery production in India notes that sodium-ion uses sodium-based cathodes and hard carbon anodes, which are relevant to localization [30]. The snippet does not provide Indian capacity figures.

## 3. Evidence Analysis

**Points of agreement.** Across the sources, the consistent picture is: hard carbon anodes, abundant sodium, lower energy density than Li-ion, and better cold and thermal behavior [1][6][7][10][17][24]. The cost case is widely invoked but is supported mainly by projections [4][12][14].

**Points of tension.**
- *Cost advantage.* Sources frame sodium-ion as cheaper, citing resource abundance [3] and projected cost reductions [12][14]. However, [14] describes parity with high-energy NMC cells in some scenarios, and [12] projects reductions in the range of roughly 10–20%, though the snippet is truncated. These are projections for mature, scaled production, not current market prices, and the sources differ in the magnitude and conditions of the advantage.
- *Maturity.* Commercialization is described as achieved in some listings [29] and as still facing mass-production barriers [16]. These are not necessarily contradictory (commercialization of some products versus scaling to EV-grade volumes), but the sources do not clarify which applies to EVs specifically.
- *Safety.* Sodium-ion is positioned as having stable thermal behavior [10], yet a thermal-runaway review emphasizes that additional challenges remain before the technology reaches Li-ion's level of safety assurance [2]. The evidence favors cautious optimism rather than settled safety superiority.

**Strength of evidence.** Most sources are journal articles accessed through abstracts or snippets. Several are very recent (2025–2026) and have low citation counts, so their conclusions are preliminary. Highly cited works (for example, [12] and [3]) are more established but mostly predate the current wave of Chinese industrialization. Only a few sources speak directly to EVs, and fewer still address India.

## 4. Data & Statistics

- **Energy density:** Sodium-ion is consistently reported as lower than LFP and NMC on energy density, but the sources in this set do not provide a verified numeric range in the retrieved text.
- **Cold operation:** Reported operability to about -20°C in a dynamic-cycle comparison [6].
- **Cost:** Projected reductions of approximately 10–20% relative to a baseline [12] and cost parity claims in specific scenarios [14]. These are modeling-based and conditional.
- **Manufacturing share:** An IISD statement that storage manufacturing capacity was less than half of global capacity and would remain so until 2025 [30]. The snippet does not clearly indicate whether this applies to sodium-ion specifically, so it should not be used as a sodium-ion statistic.

No INR-denominated pricing, Indian production capacity, or vehicle deployment figures were found.
